# GTA 6: Will Vice City Business Buying Return? — Narration Script

## WILL GTA 6 LET YOU BUY BUSINESSES?

**On screen:** Vice City's empire mechanic, explained · And whether it's coming back

Twenty-four years ago, Tommy Vercetti didn't just take over Vice City. He bought it. Club by club, cab firm by cab firm, he turned dirty money into an empire. Now Grand Theft Auto Six is taking us back to Vice City, and one question keeps coming up. Will we be able to buy businesses again? Rockstar hasn't said a word about it. So in this video, we're going to break down exactly how the original system worked, how it evolved, and what the evidence suggests about Grand Theft Auto Six.

## THE ORIGINAL: ASSETS

**On screen:** Buy a property with cash · Unlock its mission chain · Finish it → daily income

Let's start with the original. In Grand Theft Auto Vice City, back in two thousand and two, properties came in two flavours. Safehouses were simple. You paid once, you got a save point and a garage. But the real stars were the assets. Each asset was a business with a pink house icon and a price tag. Buy it, and you didn't just get a building. You unlocked a short chain of missions tied to that business. Finish the chain, and the business started generating cash every in-game day, piling up at the front door until you came by to collect it.

## THE EMPIRE

**On screen:** Boatyard — $10,000 · Cherry Popper Ice Cream — $20,000 · Pole Position Club — $30,000 · Kaufman Cabs — $40,000 · Sunshine Autos — $50,000 · InterGlobal Films — $60,000 · Print Works — $70,000 · The Malibu Club — $120,000

There were eight of them, and every one had a personality. The Boatyard was the cheapest, at ten thousand dollars. The Cherry Popper ice cream factory had you selling, let's say, more than ice cream from a van. Kaufman Cabs had you sabotaging rival taxi companies. Sunshine Autos turned you into a car thief with a shopping list. InterGlobal Films had you producing an adult movie. The Print Works was a front for counterfeit money. And the crown jewel, the Malibu Club, cost a hundred and twenty thousand dollars and unlocked a full bank heist, with a crew you recruited yourself.

## WHY IT WORKED

**On screen:** Money had a purpose · Side content = story · The ending required it

Here's why fans still talk about it. First, it gave money a purpose. Most open-world games hand you cash with nothing to spend it on. Vice City made the city itself the shop. Second, each business was really a hidden storyline, packed with its own characters and jokes. And third, it was tied to the plot. The final mission wouldn't unlock until Tommy's business empire was up and running. Building the empire wasn't a distraction from the story. It was the story. Scarface, in game form.

## HOW IT EVOLVED

**On screen:** Vice City Stories: build and defend an empire · GTA V: properties with passive income · GTA Online: the business sim era

Rockstar didn't abandon the idea. They kept iterating on it. Vice City Stories, in two thousand and six, let you seize buildings and choose what kind of racket to run in each one, then defend them from rival gangs. Grand Theft Auto Five brought back property purchases, from Los Santos Customs to a strip club to a cinema, with weekly income and occasional jobs. And Grand Theft Auto Online went all in. Bunkers, nightclubs, motorcycle clubs, arcades, agencies. Running businesses is basically the backbone of Online's entire economy, and it's made Rockstar a fortune.

## THE CLUES IN GTA 6

**On screen:** Return to Vice City and Leonida · Boobie Ike: real estate, strip club, studio · Brian Heder: properties + drug runs · Two partners chasing the American dream

So, what about Grand Theft Auto Six? Officially, nothing is confirmed. But look at the clues. The game returns to Vice City, the home of this mechanic. Lucia and Jason are a couple trying to get ahead, and the whole tone is about chasing the American dream by any means necessary. Rockstar's own character profiles lean hard into business. Boobie Ike is described as a Vice City legend who turned his street credentials into a real estate, strip club, and recording studio empire. Brian Heder lets Jason live in one of his properties, as long as he helps out with drug runs. This is a world built around people who own things.

## THE VERDICT

**On screen:** Story-tied business buying: LIKELY · Full Vice City-style asset list: POSSIBLE · Deep management sim: probably saved for Online

Here's my verdict. Some form of business ownership in Grand Theft Auto Six is very likely. It fits the setting, the characters, and the themes almost perfectly. A full return of the classic system, with a list of fronts you buy and unlock missions for, is possible, and honestly it would be the smartest nod to fans Rockstar could make. But the deep management side, the stock, the upgrades, the supply runs, will most likely be saved for the next version of Grand Theft Auto Online, where it makes money long after launch. Just remember, this is analysis, not a leak. We'll know for sure when the game is out.
